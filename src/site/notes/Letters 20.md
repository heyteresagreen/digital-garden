Hi there,

I finished knitting attempt #2 of a [top](https://www.ravelry.com/patterns/library/sabai-top) that I previously had to completely pull apart and start all over again. Because I'm still very much a newbie it took me some time to be able to see the mistake I was making, but once I knew it was impossible to un-see it (if you are a knitter then the term "rowing out" might mean something to you). It had taken me weeks to get to that point in the pattern, and it was so tempting to just keep going and hope it wouldn't look *that* bad. Part of me just really wanted to get to the end result.

![[sabai top - 1 (1).jpeg|600|Photo of my knitting part way through project]]
*Attempt #1. Spot where I figured out how to address my tension problems.*

If the point of this exercise was completely just to have a knit top to wear, I could have bought something with a click of a button without all this effort (and probably much cheaper too). But I wanted a cosy hobby, to learn to knit a garment I would actually wear, and the process is the entire point. 

From this perspective, discovering my problem and figuring out a solution (combination knitting, for any knitters out there struggling with rowing) was not a setback but a breakthrough. I had to go backwards and pull it all apart and start again, and I'm so glad I did. It still isn't perfect, but it's the most ambitious thing I've ever knitted and definitely earned its place in my wardrobe. 

I feel like there is a constant push to *go faster*, optimise, get to the finish line. Choosing to do something that is painfully slow and relishing the process is a necessary balance. Taking the time to go backwards and redo something because I've learned how to make it better, rather than racing to a mediocre finished product that would have inevitably sat in the back of a drawer unworn. There is time.

![[sabai top - 1.jpeg|400|Photo of me wearing the Sabai Top]]
*Attempt #2 - Much better knitting tension! My modelling skills could us some work!*

I think I'll put down my knitting needles and get back to painting more now that the weather is warming up again, although I keep seeing very tempting summer projects!
